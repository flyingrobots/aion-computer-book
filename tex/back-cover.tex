\noindent
We’ve spent a century pretending that computation is a neat little layer
sitting on top of reality: CPUs, stacks, algorithms, “software.” \COMPUTER{}
starts from the opposite assumption and does not back down:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Computation is the substrate.}\\
Physics, intelligence, and even ``universes'' are what certain computations look like from the inside.
\end{quote}

This book builds a full-stack ontology for that claim:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{WARP Graphs (WARP)} as the underlying state of reality:
        graphs that can contain graphs, rules, and meta-structure.
  \item \textbf{Double-pushout (DPO) rewrite} as the only law of motion:
        local graph transformations instead of differential equations or opcodes.
  \item \textbf{MRMW}---the Multi-Rule Multi-World space\emd{}as the geometry of all
        possible universes and all their histories.
  \item \textbf{Curvature, superposition, interference, measurement} as emergent
        properties of rewrite bundles and constraint resolution.
  \item \textbf{Multiversal machines} (time-travel debuggers, counterfactual engines,
        adversarial universes, cross-world optimizers) as a new class of computers
        that operate on \emph{families} of universes instead of single runs.
  \item \textbf{C$\Omega$MPILER and runtime} as an architecture that treats code,
        models, physical simulations, and institutions as first-class WARP graph universes.
\end{itemize}

Along the way, \COMPUTER{}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item reframes physics as a special region of MRMW (C$\Omega$SMOS),
  \item treats intelligence as geometry-aware navigation of that space,
  \item and forces ethics to confront the reality of machines that can create,
        manipulate, and delete entire worlds.
\end{itemize}

This is not a gentle introduction to computation.
It is a field guide for people who intend to build, steer, and survive
full-stack multiversal systems.

If you’ve ever had the feeling that “computer science,” “physics,” and “AI”
are secretly arguing about the same object with different accents—this book
is the claim that you’re right, and a proposal for that object’s name.
